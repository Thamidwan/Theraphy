What to expect at your first OA assessment

Who you'll meet, what to bring, and how your recovery goals get set.

Read the guide

Your first visit is about understanding your whole recovery, not just one problem. You'll meet the disciplines relevant to your condition — often physiotherapy and occupational therapy first, with psychology, speech therapy or dietetics added where they're needed. Together you'll agree clear, personal goals: walking further, returning to work, managing a chronic condition, or simply feeling steadier day to day. Bring your hospital discharge letter, a list of your medication, and the name of your medical aid. The assessment usually takes about an hour, and transport to and from it can be arranged.

Recovering at home after a stroke, surgery or major illness

Practical guidance for the first weeks back home.

Read the guide

The first weeks at home are where recovery is won or lost. Keep moving within safe limits, follow your exercise plan daily even when progress feels slow, and make small changes to your home — clear walkways, a shower chair, frequently used items within reach. Eat regular, protein-rich meals to help tissue heal, and watch for low mood or anxiety, which are common and treatable. OA's programme coordinates all of this, so you're not managing physiotherapy, psychology and nutrition as separate battles.

Medical aid & OA: authorisation, cover and what it costs

What's covered, how OA handles the paperwork, and options if you're not yet covered.

Read the guide

OA is accepted by most major medical aids, and our team handles the authorisation process for you — we submit the request, motivate for the sessions your programme needs, and follow up. Many plans also cover patient transport under a transport or ambulance benefit. If you're not yet on a medical aid, or your cover is limited, we accept card payments and can help you apply for a suitable plan. You'll always know what is covered before your programme begins, so cost is one less thing to worry about.

What group therapy at OA actually looks like

Peer support alongside your individual programme — not instead of it.

Read the guide

Group sessions bring together patients recovering from similar conditions — stroke, cancer, cardiac events, or mental health challenges. A therapist guides each session, but much of the value comes from the group itself: shared progress, honest conversation, and the reassurance of people who understand. Sessions run alongside your individual programme, never instead of it. Light meals and refreshments are provided, and there's a comfortable space to rest between sessions. Transport is included, the same as for individual appointments.

Frequently asked questions: transport, timing and what to bring

The practical details, answered.

Read the guide

Transport is included and arranged for you — we collect you from home and bring you back after every session. Most programmes run two to three sessions a week, scheduled around your energy levels and other appointments. Bring your discharge letter, medication list, medical aid details, comfortable clothing and any walking aid you use. Family members are welcome at assessments and can be included in planning. If you need to cancel, let us know as early as you can so we can adjust your transport.

The OA referral pathway, explained

Acute → rehab → outpatient — where OA fits, and how to start a referral.

Read the guide

OA sits at the outpatient end of the Acute → Rehab → Outpatient continuum. A referral doesn't restart care — your discharge notes and goals feed directly into the OA case plan, and the multidisciplinary team builds on your clinical work. To refer, call or WhatsApp with the patient's details and what they need; OA responds within 24 hours, handles medical aid authorisation, and arranges transport. From day one you have visibility of the programme through progress reports and case discussions.

What you receive when you refer to OA

The four-part feedback loop that keeps you in the picture.

Read the guide

Every referral opens a feedback loop with four parts: regular progress and outcome reports measured against the goals set at discharge; an open invitation to join MDT case discussions on your shared patients; a direct line to the treating therapist, with no call centre in between; and a full discharge summary at the end of the programme. The intention is simple — you should never lose sight of a patient because you referred them onward.

Cancer & mental health: why psychology referrals matter post-diagnosis

The most frequently skipped referral, and why it shouldn't be.

Read the guide

Psychological support is one of the most frequently skipped referrals after a cancer diagnosis, yet distress, anxiety and depression significantly affect treatment adherence and recovery. OA's clinical psychologists work as part of the same team managing a patient's physiotherapy, occupational therapy and dietetics, so mental health care isn't a separate appointment the patient has to seek out alone. Group programmes for cancer patients add peer support. This ties into OA's community awareness work on the importance of early psychology referral.
